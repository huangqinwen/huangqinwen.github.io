About

Hi, I’m Wendy. I like building things that make life feel a little clearer, easier, or more human.

My path hasn’t been especially linear, but curiosity has always pulled me forward. I started in biomedical engineering at Johns Hopkins, then went on to a PhD in computer science at Duke, where I worked on machine learning for cryo-electron tomography — a technique for seeing proteins inside cells. That work eventually became tools used by national labs and universities.

Over time, I became interested in how scientific and technical ideas move from research into the world. I explored venture capital, then worked at Bain on growth strategy and go-to-market questions across AI, software, and healthcare. Later, I built and sold PowerPoint automation tools after seeing how much repetitive work people quietly accept as “just part of the job.”

These days, I find myself coming back to health: how we understand our bodies, how we stay well for longer, and how better tools might help people make sense of their physical, mental, and financial lives without adding more complexity.

Outside of work, I’m usually reading, following my curiosity into new topics, or spending time with my cat, Duoduo, who is very good at reminding me to slow down.
